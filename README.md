# Speech-Emotion-Recognition---Final-Year-Thesis
Speech Emotion Recognition (SER) aims to identify emotions from speech signals using
advanced machine learning techniques. In this study, we investigate deep learning models,
including Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM)
networks, for emotion classification. Key acoustic features, such as Zero-Crossing Rate
(ZCR), Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCC), and Root-Mean Square (RMS), are
extracted from the speech data to capture essential spectral and temporal characteristics.
The dataset comprises a combination of four speech corpora—TESS, RAVDESS, SAVEE,
and CREMA-D—all in a single native language, with speech samples from speakers of
different ages, genders, and emotional states. Preprocessing steps such as noise reduction
and data augmentation techniques, including pitch shifting and time stretching, are applied
to improve data quality and model robustness. Results from experiments show that
by employing a couple of pre-trained models, an impressive performance in accurately
identifying emotions that closely resemble human references is indicated by the obtained
accuracy score of 96%, along with precision, recall, and F1–score of 96%, 96%, and 96%
respectively.
